# Naming Study: Mosaic and the Alternatives

> **Verified by the lead before you read this.** The study's load-bearing claim is that
> "mosaic" already means *pixelate a face into blocks* in the App Store's photo vocabulary.
> I checked it independently rather than taking the agents' word. It holds, and it is
> sharper than the study states: an App Store search for mosaic in a photo context returns
> blur and censorship tools almost exclusively, including one named literally
> **"Mosaic: Blur & Censor Photo"**, and another, **"Blur & Mosaic Photo Editor"**, which
> uses offline facial recognition to automatically obscure the faces it finds. That app
> detects faces in order to hide them. This one detects faces in order to keep them whole.
> Same word, opposite promise, same category.
>
> What I did NOT verify: any of the trademark readings, the domain and handle availability,
> or the rating counts attributed to competitor apps. Treat those as research leads, not
> as findings. See section 6.


Five research passes fed this: a category map of the iOS collage field, a trademark and App Store collision check on "Mosaic", an ASO read on how the name field actually earns its keep, an availability screen across 35 candidates, and an adversarial pass whose job was to kill everything. They did not agree, and where they did not I say so rather than smoothing it over.

One finding converged from three independent directions and it is the reason this study exists in its current shape. The problem with "Mosaic" is not mainly the trademark count. It is that in the App Store's photo vocabulary, "mosaic" already means **pixelate a face into blocks**. Twelve of the top thirty apps returned for the term are blur and censorship tools. Most of the rest are photomosaic generators that build one big image from thousands of tiny ones. Collage is the third meaning at best. This app's headline feature is that it does not clip faces. The name asserts the opposite of the differentiator, and no modifier repairs that, because the modifier does not change what the head noun means to a shopper.

## 1. Executive Summary

### 1. Composure

**App Store name: `Composure: Photo Collage` (24 / 30)**
**Subtitle: `Face-aware layouts, on device` (29 / 30)**
**Home screen label (CFBundleDisplayName): `Composure`**

Why it wins. It is the only candidate in the study whose ordinary meaning is the product's measured result. Twenty-two clipped faces down to two is, literally, a set of pictures with composure: nothing crowded against an edge, nothing fighting. It carries the verb (the app composes) without claiming to edit, retouch or generate, so it clears the over-claim constraint cleanly and stays honest about an app that invents nothing. It spells from hearing on the first try, survives at 11pt under an icon, and grows without limit: more photos, iPad, watermark removal, none of it touches the name. It is a common English word applied arbitrarily to photo software, which is exactly the shape that registers well in Class 9, and unlike almost everything else on the shortlist it does not point at a funded opponent. The availability screen found three App Store apps leading with the word, in Health and Fitness, Sports and Business, none in Photo and Video, and nothing adverse in a software trademark search.

What it costs, stated plainly rather than buried. **On a home screen the word reads as emotional state before it reads as composition.** The adversarial pass called this out and it is fair: the first association for some people will be meditation or sports psychology, and the layout meaning arrives second. That is a real cost and the subtitle has to do the concrete work. It is also nine characters of abstraction, so it gives Apple's search nothing to hang a photo query on by itself. Secondary costs: `composure.com` is parked and for sale (buyable, not free), `composure.app` is registered.

### 2. Terrazzo

**App Store name: `Terrazzo: Photo Collage` (23 / 30)**
**Subtitle: `Auto layouts, no cropped faces` (30 / 30)**
**Home screen label: `Terrazzo`**

Why it wins. This is the best answer to "keep the mosaic feeling, lose the mosaic problem." Terrazzo is pieces set flush into a continuous field with a visible margin between them, which is exactly what the border control produces. It is a design word the target audience already recognises and can spell once they have seen it, it sits naturally on the editorial shelf beside Halide, Darkroom and Obscura, and it exits the pixelate-a-face query pool entirely. The App Store is effectively empty: ten results for the term, and the one occupant is a flooring trade app, not a competitor.

What it costs. **`terrazzo.app` is taken by an active open-source design-token toolchain that is explicitly named for the same mosaic metaphor** and aimed at developers. Different audience from family photo users, but it will own the Google results and the obvious domain. The double-z is a coin-flip speller for a general audience hearing it cold. And it is a common material noun, which makes it descriptive-adjacent and weakly protectable relative to Composure. The positioning pass scored it 22; the adversarial pass wounded rather than killed it, on the grounds that terrazzo names a dense scatter of small chips, which is not what a two-to-four photo composition looks like.

### 3. Tessel

**App Store name: `Tessel: Photo Collage & Grid` (28 / 30)**
**Subtitle: `Face-aware layouts, on device` (29 / 30)**
**Home screen label: `Tessel`**

Why it is here. It was the only name of thirty-five that the adversarial pass left standing. Tessellation is pieces fitted edge to edge with no gap and no overlap, which is precisely what a split-tree layout does: every cell tiles the canvas exactly and the app decides where each piece sits. It keeps the owner's mosaic instinct at the level of the tile, without a single letter of the wrong-meaning word. Six characters, safe at thumbnail size, no Photo and Video conflict.

What it costs. It is a stem, not a word, so cold listeners reach for "vessel" or "Tesla" and a meaningful minority will write "Tessell". The availability screen also corrected the claim that it sits in empty space: there is an exact-name game (Gijs van Tulder, 0 ratings), TESSEL marks exist for tables and for 3D printers, and the Tessel JavaScript hardware board still has developer mindshare. None of that is in this category, so it is survivable, but it needs a spelling-recovery plan (subtitle, keyword field entry for `tessell`) and it is the least warm of the three.

**The two agents disagreed sharply here and you should know it.** The positioning pass ranked Composure first and Tessel ninth. The adversarial pass wounded Composure and declared Tessel the sole survivor. I have taken Composure first because its weakness is a second-order association a subtitle can fix, while several higher-scoring names died on collisions no subtitle can fix.

## 2. The Recommendation If You Keep "Mosaic"

**Best Mosaic-retaining option: `Mosaicist: Photo Collage` (24 / 30).**

Of everything that keeps the literal string, this is the strongest. A mosaicist is the person who decides which tessera goes where and where the seams fall, which is exactly what the engine does: template choice, photo-to-cell assignment, divider placement, all scored against detected faces. The `-ist` ending pulls the token partly clear of the censorship apps, because no blur tool calls itself a mosaicist. Availability came back Tier 1 clear: zero App Store hits, nothing adverse surfaced in any class, `mosaicist.app` free, `x.com/mosaicist` free. The positioning pass scored it 19, the best of the whole keep-Mosaic set.

Runner-up if registrability outranks pronounceability: **Mosaicon**. It is arbitrary for photo software, every existing user is non-software and non-US (Italian handbags, Polish conservation), and `mosaicon.app` is free. It has the best registration story of the Mosaic family. The cost is that a human ear parses it as "mosaic plus icon" or as a fan convention.

**The honest read: keeping the word is not defensible on this evidence.** Three separate problems, and a compound only touches the least important one.

1. **Legal exposure gets reshuffled, not removed.** A compound is probably registrable where bare "Mosaic" probably is not, which is a real gain over owning nothing. But you would own the compound, not the word, and Apple's App Name Dispute process is not a likelihood-of-confusion analysis. Mosaic S.r.l. ships *Photo Collage Maker PicJointer* (133,276 ratings) and *Split Pic Collage Maker Layout* (15,679) in this exact category, under a corporate name that is your product name. They do not need to win a lawsuit to take you off sale for three weeks. Separately, the IAC "Mosaic Group" consumer app portfolio was sold to Bending Spoons in 2024 in a deal described as including intellectual property.
2. **Branded search leaks to an incumbent.** *Mosaic - Video and Photo Collage* (Limit Point Software) has been live since 2013 in this category. Branded search is the one query you are guaranteed to win and it converts at many times the rate of generic terms. It is also the entire payoff of an Apple feature, a review, or word of mouth. Every one of those moments sends some share of people to the other app.
3. **The semantic problem, which is the worst one and which no modifier fixes.** Spaced compounds ("Magic Mosaic") tokenise straight back into the "mosaic" query pool with the blur tools: high volume, wrong intent, terrible conversion. Closed coinages ("Mosaicon") get a clean token nobody searches for, which is fine but means the word buys zero discovery. Either way the word earns nothing, and Apple's semantic search now clusters you by what the name means, not what it spells.

Two agents also flagged your own example specifically. "Magic" is not free space: the category leader ships as *PicCollage: Magic Photo Editor* (1.83M ratings), and the entire incantation vocabulary (Wand, Conjure, Spellai, Enchant) now signals AI image generation. For an app whose pitch is on-device, no server, invents nothing, magic vocabulary communicates the opposite of the truth. **"Magic Layout" is an excellent feature name, an excellent screenshot caption, and an excellent line in a featuring nomination. It is a bad app name in 2026.** Keep it exactly where it is.

If you want your vocabulary without the liability, the mosaic-family words are the move: Terrazzo (the technique), Tessel (the fitting), Andamo (the craft term for the direction of the arrangement). You keep the instinct, you get an ownable mark, and you get green space, which no Mosaic compound in this study achieves.

## 3. What the Market Looks Like

The field names itself after the **output** ("collage", "grid", "frame") or the **act of making** ("maker", "creator", "stitch"). Roughly seventy percent of the top twenty-five are literal keyword strings, four different developers ship something essentially called "Photo Collage Maker", and almost every attempt at a brand is a portmanteau built from the same three morphemes: `pic`, `collage`, `grid`. Collageable, InCollage, Encollage, PicJointer, PicGrid, Griddy. The result is a category where the branded names are mutually indistinguishable.

Saturated to invisibility: `collage` (23 of the top 25), `maker` (about 17), `pic`/`photo` as a prefix, `grid`, `frame` (Frameo owns the phonetic space at 416k ratings), `stitch`, `layout` (four separate squatters on Instagram's abandoned brand).

**Nothing in the category names itself after the composition, or after the craft of arranging.** That is the open territory, and it is open across dozens of words rather than one or two: Spread, Pane, Gutter, Salon, Tessera, Diptych, Quarto, Inlay, Terrazzo, Weave, Parquet all came back at zero Photo and Video occupancy. The playbook is proven adjacent to this category: Halide, Darkroom, Obscura, Kino, Spectre and Slidebox all take a word from the physical craft the app digitises, and Apple's editorial team features them repeatedly. The 2026 Apple Design Award list contains no keyword strings at all.

The two genuinely uncontested keyword territories this app can honestly claim are **faces** ("no cropped faces" is the user's own language for the grievance, and essentially no collage app competes there) and **the demand Instagram abandoned** when Layout was pulled.

Two things that are true whatever name you pick.

- **The "spiritual successor to Layout" story must never appear in App Store metadata.** Not the title, subtitle, keyword field, description, or screenshot text. It trips Guideline 5.2.1 and hands Meta an easy complaint. Keep it in press outreach, the website, the featuring nomination narrative, and word of mouth, where it is fair comparative speech. The word "layout" itself is safe in a descriptive tail position, never as the leading brand token.
- **The App Store name and the home screen label are separate fields.** The 30-character cap governs the store listing; `CFBundleDisplayName` governs the icon label and truncates around 11 to 12 characters. Set it deliberately to the bare brand word. Your "reads at thumbnail size" constraint is decided entirely by that second field.

## 4. The Full Shortlist

Positioning scores are out of 25, from five axes: truth to the product, distinctiveness, warmth, editorial appeal, longevity.

| **Knack** | 23 | *Knack - Tutoring Simplified* (1,915 ratings) plus three more. **At least four separate KNACK software filings** by different owners, plus knack.com, a funded no-code platform. | Killed. Nobody owns it, everybody claims it. |
| **Regard** | 23 | *Regard* by Regard, Inc., a funded AI clinical-notes company actively building the brand in software. `.com` registered, `.app` parked. | Killed. Also reads as an email sign-off. |
| **Terrazzo** | 22 | Ten App Store results, one occupant is a flooring trade app. **`terrazzo.app` taken by an active open-source design-token brand using the same metaphor.** `.com` possibly free. | **Recommended.** Domain and dev-tool collision are the price. |
| **Tessera** | 22 | **Eight leading-name apps** including *Tessera: Design Studio* and *Tessera: Seamless Patterns*. **Tessera / Adeia holds a Class 9 portfolio covering image-processing software.** | Killed. See section 5. |
| **Andamo** | 21 | Effectively invisible in the App Store (one result total for the term). No mark surfaced. All domains and handles registered; Andamo Group is a live hospitality consultancy. | Legally the cleanest coinage. Means nothing to any buyer. |
| **Dovetail** | 21 | No leading-name app, but dovetail.com is a large customer-insights SaaS holding registered marks, plus Dovetail Software and Dovetail Games. | Killed on opponent size. |
| **Headroom** | 21 | **Five leading-name apps, including *Headroom AI* in Photo and Video.** Live Class 9 registration for downloadable conferencing software. | Killed. |
| **Tessel** | 20 | Exact-name game (0 ratings), TESSEL marks for tables and 3D printers, tessel.io hardware legacy. **No Photo and Video conflict.** `x/@tesselapp` free. | **Recommended.** Spelling coin-flip is the whole risk. |
| **Mosaicist** | 19 | Tier 1 clear: zero App Store hits, nothing adverse in any class, `.app` and X handle free. `mosaicist.com` is a live architectural mosaic business. | Best keep-the-word option. Three sibilants make it hard to say and spell. |
| **Mosaic Reflex** | 17 | Cleanest availability in the entire study: zero App Store hits, **both `.com` and `.app` unregistered**, nothing adverse. | Legally spotless and strategically hollow. Users type the first word. |

**This table is where the agents disagreed most.** The availability screen's own top picks were Mosaic Reflex, Mosaic Spread and Mosaicry, on the grounds that they were the only names with zero occupancy and free domains in both TLDs. The positioning pass ranked all three in the bottom half, because legal cleanliness and market meaning were pulling in opposite directions: those names are clean precisely because nobody wants them, and they retain the branded-search leak and the wrong-meaning query pool that the study is trying to escape. I have weighted market meaning higher. If your priority is minimum legal friction rather than maximum positioning, Mosaic Reflex is the honest counter-recommendation and it is cheap to acquire.

## 5. Notable Kills

These looked good and died. Recorded so you do not relitigate them.

- **Tessera.** The candidate brief recommended it and the positioning pass scored it 22. Both were wrong on the facts. There are at least eight leading-name apps in the store, including two pattern-and-mosaic creation tools that are the nearest neighbours in the entire study. Worse, **Tessera is a live brand of Xperi/Adeia**, the imaging-IP licensor whose sibling brand FotoNation does on-device face detection and which has litigated over imaging patents. Naming a face-detecting photo app "Tessera" aims a solo developer at one of the most aggressive imaging portfolios in the industry. Strike it first.
- **Knack.** The best translation of "magic" into vocabulary that does not smell of AI generation, and it scored 23. Four separate KNACK software trademark filings by different owners, plus knack.com, a funded no-code platform with thousands of business customers, plus a Belgian newsweekly. The configuration where nobody owns a word and everybody claims it is the worst one for a solo developer.
- **Regard.** Quietly the smartest name in the study on the merits: it claims the app looks before it decides, and the looking goes nowhere, which does the privacy work for free. Killed by Regard, Inc., a funded AI clinical-notes company with press coverage and EMR distribution, and by the fact that in ordinary English the word survives mostly inside "kind regards".
- **Headroom.** Names the differentiator in the craft's own language and sets up the best subtitle in the study. Killed by *Headroom AI* sitting in Photo and Video, a live Class 9 registration held by the Headroom meetings company, and Max Headroom owning the compound in cultural memory. It also only describes vertical space, while the scorer protects faces in every direction.
- **Dovetail.** The most precise description of the split-tree mechanic anywhere on the list: adjacent cells share one moveable edge and the drag handle is the joint. Killed purely on opponent size.
- **Aglow.** The warmest name and it names the reveal directly. An exact-name app is live, Aglow International holds a registered mark, all domains are gone, and in Photo and Video the entire glow lexicon belongs to beauty filters and skin retouching, which implies the editing this app does not do.
- **Gleam.** Among the most crowded checked: *Gleam: Social Intelligence* alone has 8,456 ratings, plus gleam.io and the Gleam programming language.
- **Faceward and Facewise.** Truest names in the study. **FACEWARE is a live registered mark for "computer software for facial animation"**, one letter and one phoneme away, in Class 9. They also read as face-swap and face-edit, which is the exact AI-manipulation neighbourhood the product is trying to stand apart from.
- **Mosaica.** Confirmed live: *Mosaica AI avatars and filters* sits in Photo and Video under the same name. An AI-generation app is the worst possible neighbour for an app that invents nothing.
- **Musaic.** Heard aloud it is "music", full stop, and there is a live 2025 USPTO registration in computer and software services plus an existing audio brand. Word of mouth would convert every recommendation into a search for a music app.
- **Quarto.** Beautiful shelf-mate for Halide and Darkroom, killed by Posit's registered QUARTO for publishing software (with a published trademark usage policy) and Quarto Group the publisher, both in the document and layout space.
- **Seamly.** A direct software-to-software conflict with seamly.io and Seamly2D, against owners who already file marks. Also heard as "seemly", one letter from "seamy".
- **Spread, Salon, Easel, Inlay.** All four are bare common nouns owned by other industries: magazine spreads lose to food and finance, salon loses to hair, easel loses to painting apps and Inventables, inlay loses to dentistry. Unsearchable and unregistrable at once. This is where I side with the adversarial pass over the category map, which had proposed Spread as its strongest green-space find.
- **Fourup.** Maximum truth today, dead the day photo five ships. Never weld a current product limit into a permanent brand.
- **Mosaix, Mosaick, Mosaicry, Magic Mosaic.** Mosaix has two live apps including a photo-mosaic builder in Graphics and Design, and its `.com` sits on a corporate brand-protection registrar. Mosaick fails spell-from-hearing and still fuzzy-matches to the pixelate tools, so it pays every cost of a rename for none of the benefit. Mosaicry is the cleanest name in the entire availability screen and the ear delivers "mockery". Magic Mosaic is the floor every other candidate had to clear, and it clears nothing.

## 6. What Is Still Unverified

Do not read anything above as a clearance. Specifically:

- **No real trademark clearance was performed on anything.** USPTO's Trademark Search System exposes no usable public endpoint and returned errors to automated access; Justia, uspto.report and TMview all returned 403 or refused connections. Every trademark finding in this study came from search-engine snippets of those pages. **A paid or attorney-run US Class 9 plus Class 42 knockout search on the final one or two names is a real, unskipped step.** Composure, Terrazzo and Tessel have all been characterised, none has been cleared.
- **The "~13 leading trademark matches" figure for Mosaic could not be independently verified**, and at least three marks that inflate aggregator counts are confirmed dead or cancelled.
- **Whether Mosaic S.r.l. holds a registered MOSAIC mark** in the US or EU for collage software is the single most important open question, because they are the most likely party to file an Apple name dispute. It needs an EUIPO eSearch plus lookup and a US owner-name search.
- **Whether the Bending Spoons acquisition transferred Class 9 MOSAIC registrations** could not be confirmed from assignment records.
- **Meta's LAYOUT registration status** is unverified: whether it covers the bare word or only the composite "LAYOUT FROM INSTAGRAM", in which classes, and current status. The guidance in section 3 assumes the cautious reading.
- **App Store occupancy is US-only, top 25 by relevance, from the iTunes Search API.** That approximates Apple's ordering but is not the real ranking and does not reflect personalisation. A "no match" means no leading-name app surfaced in that window, which is strong evidence, not proof. Non-US storefronts were not screened at all, which matters for Terrazzo, Smalti and anything with an Italian or Portuguese reading.
- **Social handles were barely checked**: X and GitHub HTTP status only, for about ten names. **Instagram was not checked at all**, which is the platform that matters most for this audience.
- **"Ravenna Mosaic Company" was a real historical American mosaic firm** and no successor mark was checked. Irrelevant unless that name comes back onto the list.
- **ASO mechanics are vendor consensus, not Apple documentation.** The field weight ordering (name, then subtitle, then keyword field) is stable across sources and reliable. The widely quoted "title carries 5x the subtitle weight" is a vendor estimate with no primary source. The claim that screenshot text is now indexed is plausible but unconfirmed by Apple.
- **Company sizes and litigation histories cited in the kills** (Dovetail, Xperi, Posit, Faceware) come from secondary web sources, not from docket inspection. They are strong enough to disqualify a name for a solo developer, which is a low bar to clear, but they are not verified filings.

Renaming now is cheap and renaming after first submission is expensive, so the sequencing is: pick one, pay for a Class 9 and 42 knockout on that one name only, then update the App Store Connect record before submission.
