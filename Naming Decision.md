# Naming Decision: Photo Collage

> **Verified by the lead before you read this.** Two of the four disqualifications were
> checked independently and both hold.
>
> **Tessel is genuinely taken.** There is a live app at tessel-app.pages.dev describing
> itself as "a clean, local-first photo collage editor with no accounts, no ads, and no
> watermarks... for arranging your photos into polished layouts quickly, privately, and
> without the usual template-app clutter", on iPhone and iPad. That is a paraphrase of
> this app. Same name, same category, same positioning.
>
> **SnugFit is genuinely taken.** Xyken LLC, trading as SnugFit Solutions, ships
> SnugFit-branded apps on the App Store (SnugFit O&P 3D Scanner, id1672649360, since
> 2023; SnugFit EarCam). Medical rather than photo, but the same store and the same
> name string, and Apple's IP complaint process does not require a federal registration.
>
> **NOT verified by me:** the Warner Bros LUMOS enforcement actions, the PHOTOGRID
> registration number, and every trademark class and serial in this document. USPTO
> databases block automated access. A manual TSDR search is required before filing,
> and section 5 says which candidates most need it.


## 1. THE RECOMMENDATION

**Ship as Kumiko.** App Store title: `Kumiko: Photo Layout` (20 characters, 10 to spare in the 30-character field).

It wins because it is the only candidate whose literal meaning is the product's thesis: 組子 is Japanese joinery that interlocks existing wood strips into a lattice held by pressure alone, no nails and no glue, nothing added. "It invents nothing, it arranges what you already have" is not a tagline you would have to write for this name, it is the etymology, and it gives Apple's editorial team the craft story they reliably favour plus a ready-made visual identity (asa-no-ha lattice) for the icon and the feature card. It is also the only name on the list that gets *stronger* as the product grows, because a lattice has no natural cell count, so twelve photos on iPad is more on-brand than four on iPhone. And structurally it is the exact inverse of the Mosaic problem: nothing in the US App Store prefix-matches "kumiko", the crowded shelf sits one syllable *shorter* at "kumi" and Apple's prefix matching runs the other way, so the leading token inherits an empty shelf and a launch with any reviews at all owns that query outright.

**What it costs, stated up front.** The name teaches the user nothing on sight and has zero cold-search volume, so the subtitle, the keyword field and Apple editorial carry the entire discovery load. You cannot have kumiko.com (squatted since 1998), kumiko.app (registered, dark) or @kumiko on X or Instagram. You would ship on kumikoapp.com with @kumikoapp. The craft reading is invisible to almost everyone who will hear the word: to an American it is a woman's name, to a Japanese user it reads as a woman born roughly 1950 to 1985 and secondarily as the *Sound! Euphonium* protagonist, and to a Mandarin speaker it is a foreign sound with no meaning at all. Two people had a real fight about this and I am siding with the positioning judge, but the critic's objection stands and is worth repeating honestly: a name that needs a paragraph is not doing the job of a name.

The two things that make me confident anyway: it passes the physical tests that actually matter for a solo developer with no ad budget. Six characters, no home-screen truncation, three open syllables that survive a noisy room better than anything else here, and the likely misspelling ("Kimiko") lands on harmless noise rather than on a competitor. Five of the seven names fail that test. Kumiko does not.

## 2. THE RUNNER-UP

**Wondersmith** (`Wondersmith: Collage`, 20 characters), and only conditionally.

Its search shelf at the full token is the cleanest measured anywhere in this exercise: "wondersmit" returns zero results in the US store. Its trademark surface is unusually quiet, one live mark (WONDERSMITH ENTERTAINMENT, Class 41, a solo string-musician booking agent in New York with a gmail-address attorney). wondersmith.app is verifiably free at the registry. It survives being spoken aloud.

**What would make me switch to it:** a manual USPTO search coming back showing that Wondershare (large Chinese Class 9 photo/video software company, seven shared leading letters, identical class and category) has never policed a WONDER-formative mark, *and* a decision by you that you can live with a name that promises a maker of wonders while the product's entire claim is that it makes nothing. In 2026 "Wonder" is generative-AI naming shorthand, and the store data proves it: *Wonder - AI Art Generator*, 65,356 ratings. Every user who hears "Wondersmith, it's a photo app" reasonably expects an image generator, and you would spend your App Store description walking the name backwards.

**Where the reviewers disagreed, and how I resolved it.** The positioning judge ranked Constellate second (20 points) and Wondersmith fourth (16). The critic killed Constellate and only wounded Wondersmith. I am siding with the critic, and the reason is narrow: the positioning score measures what a word *means*, while the critic measured whether the word *survives transmission*. Your only two distribution channels are spoken recommendation and spoken editorial mention, so transmission is the binding constraint. Constellate is a rare 1611 verb sitting in the phonetic shadow of "constellation", the app does not surface until all eleven characters including the double L are typed correctly, and the measured single-L miss ("constelate") returns zero Constellate apps. There is no partial-typing rescue. The critic is right that this is not a caveat, it is the business.

If Wondershare turns out to police Class 9, be honest with yourself that this list has no second name. The critic's summary was five kills, two wounded, no survivors. In that case reopen the search rather than promote a killed candidate.

## 3. NAMES TO DROP NOW

Four have genuine disqualifying problems. Drop them today and stop spending analysis on them.

- **Tessel.** A same-name, same-category, same-positioning iOS collage app is already in open TestFlight ("Collage Maker - Tessel", local-first, no accounts, no ads, iPhone and iPad, testflight.apple.com/join/f9M5ZJmD). The bare App Store name string is separately gone to a puzzle game shipped January 2026. Both premium domains are in active commercial use by software companies on the exact name. Even if the German developer abandons, the fact that a stranger independently chose this word for an identical app in the same quarter tells you it is the obvious name, and the next person will arrive at it too. The best available outcome is a permanent tie in which every editorial mention you earn is half-collected by someone else.
- **Photogram.** PHOTOGRID is a live, renewed Class 9 registration (Reg. 4497052, Jupiter Palace Pte. Ltd., first use 2011) for digital image processing software, owned by the developer of a 116,787-rating collage app. Shared PHOTOGR-, one terminal phoneme apart, identical goods and channel. Separately, the most likely mishearing does not fail gracefully, it delivers your user to that exact competitor. And the USPTO made a prior PHOTOGRAM applicant disclaim "photo" as descriptive, then issued an office action the applicant abandoned rather than answer. Also: a photogram is a cameraless image, which is the literal opposite of your product.
- **Lumosaic.** Warner Bros. filed five actions against LUMOS-formative marks in the first half of 2026 through Fross Zelnick, two of them (LUMOSMAX, LUMOSBARI) structurally identical to yours, both defaulted. Lumos Labs separately sustained an opposition in January 2026 and killed a represented applicant's mark. You have said you cannot fund a fight, and you want editorial attention, which is the loudest possible trigger for a watch service. Worse, it keeps the exact liability you abandoned Mosaic to escape: モザイク / 모자이크 / 马赛克 is the ordinary word for censorship pixelation in the three East Asian markets. And a professional naming marketplace parsed the string as "lumos + AI", which is the one thing your app is not.
- **Snugfit.** Another iOS developer (Xyken LLC, d/b/a SnugFit Solutions) has branded SNUGFIT on this exact store since March 2023, across nine apps, with the same capitalization. Apple's IP complaint process does not require a federal registration and resolves administratively and fast, so the realistic loss is a forced rename after launch. Independent of that, the one-letter typo lands on SmugMug in your own category, "fit" is one of the most crowded tokens on the store, and the register (diaper, mattress topper, orthotic) guarantees Apple editorial never looks at you. It also argues with your own border slider, which exists to *add* gaps.

**Constellate is a separate case.** It has no legal disqualifier and no duplicate competitor, which is why the positioning judge ranked it second. I would still not ship it, for the transmission reasons above, plus the fact that you would own the name in no namespace at all (constellate.com is a live revenue business owned by a party that filed a Class 42 mark four months ago, ITHAKA holds the .org, the .app is registered, @constellate has been squatted since 2008, the GitHub org is taken). Keep it on the bench, do not build on it.

## 5. WHAT A HUMAN STILL HAS TO CHECK

**Trademark databases blocked automated access across every candidate.** USPTO TSDR now requires a registered API key, tmsearch.uspto.gov is a session-gated SPA, and Justia, Trademarkia and uspto.report all returned 403 or Cloudflare challenges. Where a search "found nothing", that is frequently the absence of a *reachable* record, not the absence of a mark. **A manual TSDR and tmsearch session is required before you file anything, and for Kumiko it should happen before you commission the icon and buy the domain.** The candidates that most need it, in order: Kumiko, Wondersmith, then (only if you reconsider them) Snugfit and Constellate.

**Kumiko, the live recommendation:**
1. Attorney phonetic knockout search in Classes 9 and 42: KUMIKO against QUMICO, CUMICO, KUMICO, KOOMIKO, KUMMIKO, COOMIKO. Only a basic "contains kumiko" wordmark search was run. This is the item that would move Kumiko from PROCEED WITH CARE to CLEAR for a US launch, and it is worth a few hundred dollars.
2. TTAB / TTABVUE: no opposition or cancellation search was run for any KUMIKO mark.
3. USPTO assignment records and Kaneka's Section 8/15 maintenance status on SN 87660714 were not pulled. New filings run several months behind indexing.
4. State registers: none searched, in any state. Specifically unknown whether Kumiko (the Chicago bar) holds an Illinois registration or a recent unindexed federal application.
5. No PACER or docket search on any live holder. The read that none is aggressive is inference from filing profiles.
6. EUIPO and UKIPO Classes 9 and 42 not enumerated (TMview rate-limited). Required if an EU or UK launch is planned.
7. Japan: JPO has a **live registered KUMICO in Classes 3 and 9** (app 2018165453, Voyage Inc.). Neither that file nor the dead KUMIKO Class 9 filing (app 2024068708, Takusu Inc.) was pulled. A Japanese-reading human must clear this before a JP release.
8. Siri, on hardware, not the simulator: put a contact named "Kumiko" on a physical iPhone with a dummy app of that name installed, then run "Hey Siri, open Kumiko" and the bare "Hey Siri, Kumiko" in both en-US and ja-JP. No Apple documentation describes how the "open" intent arbitrates between an app name and a contact name. US exposure is tiny (78 US-born babies named Kumiko, 1880 to 2023); Japanese exposure is real. Note the project's own history here: the simulator already lies about Vision.
9. On-device App Store search: type "kumi", "kumik", "kumiko" and "kimiko" into the real App Store app on an iPhone signed into the US store and screenshot each shelf. Only US and JP storefronts were checked at all; GB, DE, FR, BR, KR, CN and the Mac App Store were not.
10. Domains and handles: re-verify kumikoapp.com, trykumiko.com, kumikophoto.com and kumiko.photo at the registrar on the day you buy, and buy the day you decide. Run a registrar whois on kumiko.app (registrant, expiry and whether the holder would sell are unknown; the .app registry whois would not answer). Get a price on kumiko.io if you care (it is listed on Afternic). Instagram handle availability for @kumikoapp, @getkumiko and @kumiko_app is **unverified**: Instagram returned HTTP 200 for every handle probed, so that check produced no signal and a human must look while logged in. X handles @kumikoapp, @getkumiko and @kumikophoto were control-tested and appear free. Threads, TikTok, YouTube, Bluesky, Reddit and a GitHub org were not checked at all.

**Wondersmith, if you want it as a live option:**
1. **Wondershare first, before anything else.** Search their Class 9 portfolio, their opposition history, and whether they police WONDER-formative marks. This is the flip condition.
2. No knockout or clearance search was run: no phonetic search, no design-mark search, no Class 9 or 42 similar-mark analysis, no coordinated-class sweep.
3. TTAB not checked for Juliette M. Jones or any Wonder-family owner. Supplemental Register not checked separately.
4. Zero international coverage: no EUIPO, UKIPO, WIPO Madrid, CIPO, JPO or KIPO. Given an active Japanese "Wonder Smith" figure brand and a Japanese holder of the bare X handle, a JPO check is not paranoid.
5. State registers not checked, notably Oregon (Miss Wondersmith) and North Carolina (wondersmith.tv).
6. Common-law priority not assessed. Miss Wondersmith's first use is at least January 2017, predating anything you could file, and she is one free Apple IP complaint away from being your problem even without a registration.
7. Apple's developer-name namespace not checked for an existing "Wondersmith" developer.
8. wondersmith.app availability is well verified (registry RDAP 404 against two 200-returning controls) but re-check at purchase. Instagram @wondersmithapp showed "Profile isn't available", which Instagram also shows for deactivated and banned accounts, so that is not confirmation.
9. Siri not tested on a device. JA, KO and ZH readings are reasoned from loanword convention, not native-speaker confirmed.

**Cross-cutting, applies to whatever you pick:**
- Every App Store finding in this exercise comes from the iTunes Search API, which is not the ranking engine users see and does not reproduce Apple's on-device prefix and type-ahead behaviour. Reproduce the key queries on a real iPhone before committing.
- No Siri test was performed on hardware for any of the seven names.
- No candidate had its non-US storefronts systematically checked, and Google Play was checked only incidentally.
- Nobody has run the cheapest and most informative test available: say the name aloud once to ten people and have them type it. That single test resolves the highest-confidence risk on Kumiko, Constellate and Wondersmith for the price of ten text messages.
- One free document worth pulling even though Photogram is dropped: the non-final office action issued 2026-01-05 against serial 99284002 (Alice Camera's PHOTOGRAM for image-editing software, expressly abandoned six weeks later without a response). It states exactly why the USPTO refuses a photo-prefixed mark in this category, and it is a useful calibration on how thin "photo"-leading names are.

**One last thing neither reviewer let go of, and I will not either.** All seven names describe the geometry: lattice, mosaic, tessellation, constellation, fit, grid. Geometry is the commodity part; PicCollage has done rectangles for 1.8 million ratings' worth of users. Not one of these names gestures at the actual differentiator, which is that the app looks at your photographs and sees the people in them. Kumiko is the best word on the table and I stand behind the recommendation, but the subtitle, the screenshots and the first line of the App Store description have to carry "it sees the faces", because the name will not.
