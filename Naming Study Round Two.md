## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Round two did not overturn round one. It found a better word for round one's own idea, and it rescued round one's leader from a kill that turned out to be based on two dead listings.

Plainly stated: **round one identified the correct territory and two of its three names still sit in the top three.** What changed is which word leads.

### 1st. JOINERY

**`Joinery: Photo Collage`** (22 characters)
Longer variant if the layout keyword is wanted: **`Joinery: Collage & Layout`** (25). Note `Joinery: Photo Collage & Layout` is 31 and overruns the cap by one character.

**Why it wins.** Joinery is the craft of fitting cut pieces edge to edge so tightly that no fastener and no gap is needed. That is a plain-English description of a split-tree layout, and it stays true at two photos or twelve, so it satisfies the no-count rule by construction and the unstated sibling rule (no implied fixed shape) as well. It is a real English word, spelled correctly on first hearing, with one pronunciation. Its first syllable is *join*, which is what the app does to the photos and to the people in them. It promises craft and fit, never creation, so it cannot overclaim AI, editing, or cloud. It sits in Halide and Darkroom's register as a craft-trade noun, which is the naming convention Apple's editorial team actually rewards. And it is the cleanest availability result of the entire round: one leading-name match on the US App Store (`Joinery: Daily Crosswords`, Games, zero ratings) and **zero hits in Photo & Video**.

**What it costs, stated up front rather than buried.** Three real things.
1. **First read is furniture.** For roughly half a second under an icon, it says woodworking. The mitigation is that the subtitle is always visible and the misread flatters the app instead of contradicting it, which is the opposite of the Mosaic failure, where the misread is the opposing promise.
2. **The trademark class is unresolved.** JOINERY, Joinery Connections Inc., serial 98292597, exists. The class could not be retrieved (uspto.report blocks automated fetch). This needs a manual TSDR look before filing. "Joinery" is also generic trade vocabulary in the UK, which was flagged and not verified, so file with a design element rather than as a bare word mark.
3. **A clone-tier phonetic neighbourhood.** `PhotoJoiner`, `Pic Merger: Pic Joiner`, `Photo Joiner - Photo Editor` are all live. This cuts both ways: useful keyword adjacency to real shopper intent, slight dulling of the premium register. Net positive in my judgment, but he should know it exists.
4. It names the mechanism, not the feeling. Composure names the result.

### 2nd. COMPOSURE

**`Composure: Photo Collage`** (24 characters)

**Why it is still here, and why this is the most important reversal of the round.** The adversarial pass killed it as its headline finding, on the grounds that a photo collage app named Composure by StuckPixel and a `Composure Camera` both existed in category. The availability screen then queried the live US store directly and found **both delisted**: `id656377653` (StuckPixel, a Mac App Store listing) not found, `id6471194287` (Composure Camera) returns 404. **Zero Photo & Video name hits for "composure" today.** The only trademark surfaced is COMPOSURE held by CFL Holding Limited for floor tiles and engineered stone, an unrelated class. The kill does not hold on current store data.

Its real cost is now measured rather than intuited. The living "Composure" vocabulary in the App Store is Health and Fitness: `Composure: Anger & Anxiety`, `Grounded: Regain Composure`, `Composure: Founder Protocol`. So round one's stated cost, that it reads as emotional state before composition, is confirmed by store data. **Critically, this is not the Mosaic problem.** Mosaic's competing meaning sits on the same shelf and promises the opposite thing. Composure's competing meaning sits in a different category and makes no claim about photos at all. Same shape, an order of magnitude less dangerous.

It contains "compose," its ordinary meaning is the app's measured result (22 to 2, 29 to 2), it is ownable, and it never ages out as photo counts grow. It is the strategic complement to Joinery: Joinery names the act, Composure names the result.

**What it costs.** The subtitle carries the entire concrete load, because the name alone reads as an inner state of the operator. And a delisting is not an abandonment of rights: StuckPixel shipped a same-named collage app in this exact category and may retain common-law claims. That is a smaller risk than the adversarial pass thought, but it is not zero.

### 3rd. DOVETAIL

**`Dovetail: Photo Collage`** (23 characters)

**Why it places.** It is the same truth as Joinery expressed in plainer speech: in ordinary English "dovetail" already means things fitting together perfectly, and as a joint it is the one that interlocks pieces so they cannot pull apart. No count, no implied grid, no editing or AI promise, and it spells from hearing. Store field is empty: zero leading-name matches, zero Photo & Video hits, and the social photo app `id6476598217` is delisted.

**What it costs, and where the agents disagreed.** The adversarial pass called Dovetail an outright kill: dovetail.com is a funded user-research SaaS with software-class marks and total search dominance, which it framed as exactly the unwinnable fight a solo developer must avoid. The availability screen looked at the filing and found DOVETAIL, Dovetail Research Pty. Ltd., serial 97639519, **scoped to transcription and customer-data analysis services**, not consumer photo software, and rated it a genuine different-class coexistence bet with an empty store. Both readings are defensible. It is a bet, not a clean field, and it is the one name in the top three whose risk is legal rather than perceptual. Eight letters of soft consonants also read slightly quiet at thumbnail size.

**Honourable mention, not top three:** `Inlay: Photo Collage` (20 characters). Precise, short, sharp at thumbnail, one Games match, zero Photo & Video hits, no software mark surfaced. It loses to its lane-mates on warmth and because it is one keystroke from "overlay," which reads as a developer component.

## 2. THE MAGIC VERDICT

**Not viable. Kill the whole territory. The single best name in it is `Magic Fit`, and it is still not shippable.**

This was checked independently by four agents, including two live App Store catalogue queries, and they agree without a dissent. The evidence:

**The word does not mean what he wants it to mean.** In App Store photo vocabulary in 2026, "magic" means *the app adds or removes pixels*. Google shipped Magic Eraser and Magic Editor. Canva shipped Magic Studio, Magic Edit, Magic Expand, Magic Grab. The store's long tail follows: `Magic Eraser - Remove Object`, `Magic Editor - AI Photo App` (object removal, body reshaping), `Magic Retouch`, `Magic Cam - Face Photo Editor`, `BEAM: Magic AI Photo Editor`, `MagiCut`. Every single one is generative or destructive. There is no app in Photo & Video where "magic" means thoughtful arrangement.

**The direct competitor already owns it, in the name and not the subtitle.** `PicCollage: Magic Photo Editor`, Cardinal Blue Software, **1,826,627 ratings**, last updated 2026-07-27. Round one's brief believed "Magic" was in PicCollage's subtitle. It is in the App Store name. Worse, the **same listing ID (448639966) has also run as `PicCollage: AI Photo Editor`**. The category leader treats Magic and AI as interchangeable synonyms on one product. That is not an interpretation, it is the incumbent's own metadata.

**This is the Mosaic failure discovered a second time.** Mosaic already means "pixelate the face." Magic already means "regenerate the photo." This app detects faces in order to keep them whole and never alters a pixel. Same shelf, opposite promise, twice. It also violates the owner's own hard constraint (must not imply AI image generation) in public, on the store page, before anyone reads a subtitle. The commercial damage is concrete: a shopper trained by Google and Canva taps "Magic" expecting object removal, cannot find it, and leaves a one-star review saying the app does not work.

**`Magic Grid` specifically is a double kill.** "Magic" promises generative AI the app deliberately does not do, and "grid" promises the uniform fixed cells the app is explicitly better than. The engine's entire value is moving dividers **off** centre into unequal cells. It fails the owner's own no-count rule under the sibling clause, and I would flag it even if that rule had not been written.

**There is nothing to own, and something to lose.** "Magic" is laudatory and descriptive, so you get no protection: you cannot stop the next twenty Magic Collage apps from crowding your search results. Meanwhile MAGIX Software GmbH holds a phonetically identical registered mark in the photo and video editing software class, and Wizards of the Coast fences the bare word with roughly 110 Class 9 registrations. That is a wall to file into, not a field.

**Two supporting signals worth his attention.** Apple had the obvious opening to call its iOS 18 object remover "Magic Eraser" and named it **Clean Up** instead. Apple's photo vocabulary contains no "magic" at all. And Apple Design Award winners from 2024 through 2026 contain zero apps with Magic in the name; the craft-app convention Apple rewards is Halide, Darkroom, Procreate, Play, Dredge. That convention is where Joinery, Terrazzo and Tessel sit, and where Magic Grid does not. Separately, the Magic cohort's reviews cluster around subscription complaints and trial-to-charge traps, which is the exact opposite reputation to a $2.99 one-time unlock.

**Where the word actually belongs.** Google, Canva and Apple all put the magic on the *feature* and never on the product. Keep **`Magic Layout`** as the customer-facing name of the reveal: the button, the onboarding line, screenshot one's caption, the first line of the description. It is genuinely a good feature name, it is unclaimed as a button label, and in-app the user has just watched it and has earned the word. It is only fatal on the icon.

**Adjacent enchantment vocabulary was mapped and produced nothing better.** Spellwork (Rune, Charm, Hex) reads fantasy-game. Alchemy implies transformation of the source material, the exact false promise. Wonder and Sparkle are cutesy, and "sparkle" is now the visual shorthand for AI everywhere. The only habitable band was intuition (Knack, Keen, Canny), and its best member, Knack, scored 19 and is squeezed three ways: a live Sony mark, Evenly Odd Inc's knack.com, and Knack now marketing itself as an **AI app builder**, which drags the AI freight back in through the door you just closed.

## 3. WHAT CHANGED FROM ROUND ONE

**Genuinely new:**

- **Joinery, Dovetail, Inlay and Ashlar.** Round one found the right idea and expressed it as a stem (Tessel). Round two found the English words for the same idea. This is the round's real contribution.
- **Composure's kill and its reversal.** The adversarial pass found a same-category collage app named Composure; the availability screen found it and the camera app both delisted from the US store. Net effect: Composure is *cleaner* than round one believed, and its cost is a different-category vocabulary problem, not a name collision.
- **Tessel got worse, not better.** Round one logged "an exact-name game." There are three (`Tessel`, `Tessel Run`, `Tessel: Strategy Duel`), plus `TesselMed`, plus a decade of SEO from the Tessel JavaScript microcontroller platform, plus **TESSELL, Tessell Inc., serial 98403944, Class 042 software services, active**. The predicted "Tessell" misspelling now has a destination: a database company.
- **Terrazzo got better, not worse.** Zero App Store name matches, the Terrazzo wallpaper generator is delisted, no software Class 9 mark found, and terrazzo.app is an MIT-licensed open-source design-token toolchain by an individual, not a business with a mark. Round one over-priced this collision. It is still third in its lane because it names the finished surface rather than the act, and it carries a 2018-2022 interiors trend date stamp.
- **Alight is dead and must be recorded as dead.** `Alight Motion` (Photo & Video, 70,646 ratings, now owned by Bending Spoons) plus `Alight Mobile` (285,526 ratings). Neither round one nor the warmth scout caught it.
- **Flourish is dead.** FLOURISH is a registered mark of Canva UK Operations Ltd, acquired February 2022. You would be renting the word from the company that defined magic-equals-generative-AI.
- **Poise is dead.** Poise is Kimberly-Clark's incontinence pad brand. Nobody said this in round one.
- **Fold is dead.** `Unfold: Reels & Story Maker`, Squarespace, 158,436 ratings, a layout-and-template app in the same category whose name is your name plus two letters.
- **Mantel is contested, not clear.** Two live apps plus **MANTEL MOMENTS, serial 99319513, covering downloadable mobile application software for aggregating, organizing and curating personal digital media including photos**. That is a live Class 9 filing for photo-organizing phone software using the word. The earlier "unrelated trading-card app" characterisation was wrong.
- **The count rule has a sibling.** Any name implying a *fixed shape* (Grid, Tile, quad) fails for the same reason a number fails, because the engine's whole value is moving dividers off centre into unequal cells. Screen future names against "does this imply regularity," not only "does this imply a number." Note this also means round one's suggested subtitle `Tessel: Photo Collage & Grid` should drop the "& Grid."
- **The warm register was explored properly and mostly blocked.** The single best metaphor available for this app is **Quilt**, and it is taken by Chatbooks as "the smart photo journal for families." Record that so it does not resurface in a later round. Hearth, Kindred, Reunion, Locket and Potluck are likewise occupied by adjacent family products.

**Confirmed from round one:**

- Mosaic must go. Nothing weakened that finding.
- The tiling-and-fitting family is the correct territory. All six of the strongest candidates across both rounds (Tessel, Terrazzo, Inlay, Ashlar, Dovetail, Joinery) express one concept: pieces fitted flush with nothing important cut at the seam.
- Composure remains an excellent name and a legitimate fallback, with the emotional-state cost intact and now measured.
- Bare English adjectives are a dead end. Keen, Deft, Canny and Snug all fail the same two tests at once: descriptive adjectives are weak to unregistrable as marks, and in store search they collide with every competitor's marketing copy. An adjective is a claim about the app, not a name for it.

## 5. NOTABLE KILLS

**Killed for depending on a photo count or implying a fixed shape (the owner's explicit instruction, plus its sibling clause):**

- **Quarto** and **4-Up** from round one. Automatic kill: the app already does two to four photos and may support more.
- **Grid**, in any construction including **Magic Grid**. A grid is a lattice of equal cells. The engine's differentiator is unequal cells with dividers moved off centre. "Grid" describes the thing this app is better than.
- **Tile**, same reason.
- The trailing **"& Grid"** in round one's suggested `Tessel: Photo Collage & Grid` subtitle. Drop it even if Tessel is chosen.

**Killed on trademark or a large in-category incumbent:**

- **Alight.** `Alight Motion`, Photo & Video, 70,646 ratings, Bending Spoons. Plus Alight Mobile, 285,526 ratings. Same category, enormous, litigious owner. Record as permanently dead.
- **Flourish.** Registered mark of Canva UK Operations Ltd.
- **Fold.** `Unfold`, Squarespace, 158,436 ratings, a layout app. Plus Fold Inc.
- **Every Magic construction**, including **Magic Fit** and **Magic Layout as an app name**. MAGIX holds a phonetically identical mark in the photo/video software class; PicCollage ships as "Magic Photo Editor" with 1.8M ratings; the word is descriptive so nothing is ownable.
- **Canny.** CANNY, Canny Inc., serial 98122395, a live Class 9 mark on the bare word. The ear also supplies "uncanny," the worst adjacent phrase possible for a face-detection product.
- **Limelight.** Twenty years of CDN software SEO, mark still commercially live post-Edgio bankruptcy, and it means fame-seeking.

**Killed on how it sounds or what it says:**

- **Hang.** In software, "the app hangs" means it froze. One-star reviews pun themselves. No test needed.
- **Sleight.** Heard aloud it is "slight," meaning flimsy or an insult. No subtitle repairs a name that sounds like a put-down.
- **Deft.** One vowel from "daft" to a British or Irish ear, and a bare adjective with the warmth of a compiler flag.
- **Reveal.** Bare common verb, and in 2026 consumer usage it means gender reveal. A screenshot caption, never an icon.
- **Poise.** Kimberly-Clark's incontinence brand, plus a store field that is overwhelmingly wellness and dating coaching.
- **Everyone.** The truest name in the set and the least ownable. Unsearchable by construction, it is also the App Store's age-rating word, and `Everyone: Capture Stories` already exists in Photo & Video.
- **Sampler.** For a free app with a paid unlock, the name literally reads as "the free trial version."

**Killed on overclaim, which matters because it is the same failure mode as Mosaic:**

- **Heirloom.** "Made to be handed down" is a durability and preservation promise from an app with no cloud, no sync and no backup.
- **Bindery.** Implies sequencing, paging, binding, printing. The app exports one JPEG.
- **Trove.** Names what you already have, not what the app does, and three Photo & Video apps already use it including `Trove - Family Memories`.
- **Ember.** Realmac's Ember was an iOS and Mac image-collection app that already burned this name in this space, plus 19 leading matches. An ember is also a fire going out.

**Recorded so it does not resurface:** **Quilt** is the single best metaphor for this app and it is taken by Chatbooks as a family photo journal. Do not let a later round rediscover it as if it were open.

## 6. WHAT IS STILL UNVERIFIED

Do not read this report as a clearance opinion. It is store data plus public trademark search. No lawyer looked at any of it.

**Trademark depth, the largest gap:**

- **JOINERY, serial 98292597, Joinery Connections Inc.: the class was not retrieved.** uspto.report returns 403 to automated fetch. This is the top recommendation's single unresolved risk and needs a manual TSDR lookup in classes 9 and 42 before anything is filed.
- **No EUIPO or UKIPO search was run for any candidate.** This matters specifically for Joinery, where "joinery" is generic UK carpentry trade vocabulary and distinctiveness for a bare word mark is doubtful. File with a design element.
- The **KEEN** footwear position is unverified. The claim that KEEN's marks sit in apparel and not software could not be confirmed and should not be treated as cleared.
- **A delisted app does not extinguish rights.** StuckPixel shipped a collage app named Composure in this exact category. It is gone from the US store today; that is not evidence of abandonment. Same caveat applies to `Composure Camera`, the Terrazzo wallpaper generator, and the delisted Dovetail social photo app.
- The Dovetail assessment is a **disagreement, not a finding.** One agent read dovetail.com's mark as an unwinnable fight for a solo developer; another read serial 97639519 as scoped to transcription and analytics services, a legitimate different-class coexistence. Both are defensible on the evidence gathered. Resolve with a real search if it advances past third place.

**Not checked at all:**

- **Social handles, for all 33 candidates.** Nothing in this report says anything about whether @joinery, @composure or @dovetail are available anywhere.
- **Non-US App Store storefronts.** All catalogue queries were US only.
- Domains were checked but not evaluated for acquisition cost. joinery.app is registered and parked on Afternic, meaning listed for sale. joinery.com has been held since 1997. keen.app is the only unregistered domain found in the entire round.

**Untestable with the methods used:**

- The "Hang" freeze connotation could not be empirically tested. It is treated as untested, not cleared, and the name is killed on judgment.
- Joinery's furniture-first-read cost is a judgment call, not a measurement. **The one cheap test worth running before committing: say `Joinery: Photo Collage` aloud to five people who have not seen this report, and ask them what the app does.** If "woodworking" sticks after they hear the subtitle, fall back to Composure, which is unchanged in strategy and still excellent. Joinery names the act; Composure names the result. Either is a defensible ship.
